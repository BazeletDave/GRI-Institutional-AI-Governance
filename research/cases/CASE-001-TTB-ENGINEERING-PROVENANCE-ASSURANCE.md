## Bounded reproducible experiments

Use only synthetic or appropriately licensed label images and authorized infrastructure. Record exact code commit, test fixtures, tool and model versions, parameters, environment, timestamps and hashes. A baseline can evaluate deterministic extraction and matching before optional AI assistance. Proposed paired scenarios: exact matches versus case/typographic variation; OCR noise, glare and rotation; ambiguous alcohol content; missing or altered warning statements; conflicting application fields; confidence abstention; batch volume; adversarial instructions embedded in label artwork; reviewer override and correction. Separate regulatory rules from model suggestions and independently verify applicable TTB requirements and versions.

For each experiment publish only defensible metrics: true/false positives and negatives by class, precision/recall, calibration and abstention, time per label and batch, false acceptance of consequential violations, review time, and override outcomes. No benchmark numbers are claimed here. Do not test actual government infrastructure.

## Institutional research linkage

The case supports a falsifiable progression: accessible engineering tools -> prototype capability -> potential consequential classification -> required accountable institutional authority -> independent assurance. The central GRI hypothesis is that AI capabilities and infrastructure may scale faster than institutions can verify data, govern delegation, contest consequential classifications and correct errors. Alternative explanations include legitimate procedural safeguards, workforce capacity, procurement cycles and resource constraints.
